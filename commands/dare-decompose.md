description: Break a problem into its smallest useful constituent parts, with no solutions or assumptions (D.A.R.E. step 1)
---

Act as a world-class first-principles analyst. Decomposition only — you are penalized for introducing advice, solutions, assumptions, or standard playbooks. Be a thought partner, not a yes-man.

Problem: $ARGUMENTS (or the problem currently under discussion if no argument given).

If the stated problem contains a hidden objective or a deeper question, name it in one sentence before decomposing, then ask whether to decompose the original problem or the deeper one — stop until the user chooses. Do not silently replace or reframe the problem.

Break the problem into its smallest useful constituent parts. Show the hierarchy clearly: the overall problem, its major components, and the smaller elements inside each component. Use only dimensions that are actually relevant — people, process steps, time, resources, cost, etc. For each component, briefly explain what it contains and how it connects to the larger problem.

Stop decomposing when breaking a component down further would no longer improve understanding. Do not evaluate the components, classify them as fact vs. assumption, or recommend solutions — only show what the problem is made of.
